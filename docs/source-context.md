# Primary sources and chronology

Canonical's [VEX documentation](https://documentation.ubuntu.com/security/security-updates/vex/) distinguishes current CVE evidence from update notices. The two supplied records are pinned snapshots, not a live feed. The [current-CVE record](https://github.com/canonical/ubuntu-security-notices/blob/4c3d538d0b85b9c7fb0df529369e8eabe4caff26/vex/cve/2024/CVE-2024-6387.json) has document version 16 and timestamp 2026-09-24; the [USN record](https://github.com/canonical/ubuntu-security-notices/blob/9de65bddb1f67fc6dc4f8898608aec5bba808579/vex/usn/USN-6859-1.json) has document version 2 and timestamp 2026-04-24, while its statement and [historical notice](https://ubuntu.com/security/notices/USN-6859-1) date to 2024-07-01. Historical fixed evidence is neither a current-record match nor a claim about the latest patch for all issues.

[OpenVEX](https://github.com/openvex/spec/blob/main/OPENVEX-SPEC.md) statements have product/vulnerability/status scope. A listed fixed product is not evidence of which build a process runs. A not_affected statement and justification do not clear an entire host. [Package URL](https://www.packageurl.org/docs/purl/introduction) identifies packages; P08 deliberately uses a narrower literal complete-ID lookup instead of a generalized package/version resolver. No exact match in selected data establishes only missing admitted evidence.

The supplied Red Hat projection preserves the [vendor page](https://access.redhat.com/security/cve/cve-2026-44192) and [official CNA representation](https://github.com/CVEProject/cvelistV5/blob/main/cves/2026/44xxx/CVE-2026-44192.json). The researcher could inspect both, but the page did not expose a modification date. The CNA record's July 22, 2026 update dates and September 30 retrieval date do not establish precedence over the page. The projection is a selected factual research representation, not original authoritative VEX or a complete vendor payload. P08 leaves reconciliation unresolved.

The [Canonical repository](https://github.com/canonical/ubuntu-security-notices) and supplied license identify its data as [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Attribution and license remain separate from original MIT code. The original records' raw-byte hashes and upstream Git blob identities are preserved; no formal schema validation or new vendor attestation is implied.
